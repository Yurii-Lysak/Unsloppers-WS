# People Management Platform

## Why

An engineering organisation of 500+ employees, distributed across departments and projects, needs a platform to replace/upgrade its current internal people-management system — decomposed into a strict per-section access model, with two distinct manager tiers (reporting line vs. project line) and a separate full-profile-access grant, so that who-sees-what-about-whom is never a footnote (spec §1–3). This is also the vehicle for an AI-native SDLC bootcamp: learning a clean, spec-driven, genuinely parallel delivery process is the primary objective, and a working product is the deliberate side effect — where the two conflict, process wins (spec §1). That priority is why this SPEC, and the BMad artifacts built on top of it, matter as much as the code.

## Capabilities

- **CAP-1 — Access Control, Roles & Employee Profile** *(Epic 1, incl. Profile Sharing 4.8, Population/Departments/Auth 4.17)*
  - **intent:** Resolve every viewer's audience per subject — Self / Reporting line / Project line / PP / Colleague / Shared Link / Full access — from the transitive closure of reports-to, department-management, and project-assignment relationships (reporting line = the first two, project line = the third and narrower per `access-model.md`); assemble each profile from exactly the sections that audience is entitled to; enforce the colleague whitelist and its sole campaign-sender exception, plus the S7 manager/PM note-visibility exception; gate the four organisational-relationship fields (manager, PP, department, department's manager) behind a dedicated *change organisational relationships* permission with no self-assignment, journaled; let HR Admin define and grant functional roles/permissions as runtime data with no deploy, while HR Admin itself grants no data access; operate Full profile access as its own non-self-assignable, always-at-least-one-holder grant (holder count re-checked at commit time, never from a stale read), independent of any functional role; resolve a viewer holding more than one audience for the same subject as the union of all resolved audiences' section access, per section, never a ranked precedence; let a manager (or an automatic resourcing trigger) generate a time-limited, revocable, named-recipient-only shareable profile link whose exposed sections continuously re-clamp to the creator's currently-held access on every view (not just at creation) and whose revocation/journal rights follow the current relationship holder, not its creator; and treat the seeded population (delivered via the timetracker test environment, §4.17) as the sole identity anchor, with no in-app employee-provisioning flow and no SSO.
  - **success:** Every audience × relationship-path × section combination — including the narrowed project-line cells, the multi-audience union case, and the campaign-sender exception — is covered by a negative test asserting the API/UI/export/search never returns a `—` cell, a narrowed-cell field, an unflagged S7 record, or a non-whitelisted colleague field; a new functional role created via UI is assignable and enforced with zero code change, and never widens data access on its own; an organisational-relationship change is rejected outright if it would create a self-managed department, a reporting cycle, an orphaning clear, or a lost concurrent write, and — together with a full-access grant/revocation — is provably non-self-assignable and appears in the relationship journal; a shared link's exposed sections are proven to shrink on the next view the instant its creator's access narrows, without a separate revoke step; access is re-resolved per request, with platform-owned relations effective on the next request and project assignment effective within 15 minutes.

- **CAP-2 — All Employees Directory & Custom Fields** *(Epic 3)*
  - **intent:** One filterable/sortable employee list, scoped per viewer's access, where any built-in (including employment status), derived, or custom field is usable as a column/filter/sort key; custom fields are defined at runtime by whoever holds the *manage custom fields* permission, with no schema migration; list cells are inline-editable subject to the access matrix, excluding the manager/PP/department fields (which route only through CAP-1's dedicated relationship-change screen); filter+column configurations save as named shareable views; the current view exports to `.xlsx` containing only columns the exporter can see.
  - **success:** A newly created custom field is immediately usable as filter/column/export with no deploy; a saved view is reusable and shareable; an attempt to inline-edit manager/PP/department is rejected; the list responds within 2s at 500+ records under arbitrary filters, including permission resolution.

- **CAP-3 — Self-Service** *(Epic 2)*
  - **intent:** An employee can view their own employment summary, edit their own personal/emergency contacts and residential address, upload a photo and certificates, and view their own timeline, leaves (full own detail per S10, linking out to the timetracker for balance management — balances are never shown in-platform), projects, CDS/IDP, mentorship status, shared feedback, flagged management notes, and action items — marking IDP items and action items complete themselves.
  - **success:** An employee completes every listed self-service action without manager/PP involvement, never sees their own risk level or an unflagged management note, and never sees a leave balance rendered in-platform.

- **CAP-4 — Action Items and Tasks** *(Epic 4)*
  - **intent:** One task entity (title, description, assignee, author, due date, optional link, status, completion date, source) creatable manually by a manager/PP/permitted role for anyone in their access scope, creatable automatically by form-campaign activation (one per recipient), completable by the assignee, cancellable by the author with a reason (or auto-cancelled with reason "departed" by CAP-14, idempotently — a cancellation write against an already-cancelled item is a no-op regardless of which cancellation path arrives second), rendered overdue everywhere it appears once its due date passes. Visibility follows S14, with exactly one exception: a campaign author sees their own campaign's item statuses even for otherwise-Colleague recipients.
  - **success:** Lifecycle open→completed is correct for both manual and campaign-sourced items; overdue derivation is identical everywhere it's displayed; cancellation is scoped by historical authorship, not live access; a concurrent manual cancellation and departure-triggered auto-cancellation never overwrite each other's reason/cancelled-by field regardless of arrival order; the campaign-sender exception never leaks any other S14 fact.

- **CAP-5 — Risks and Risk Dashboard** *(Epic 5)*
  - **intent:** Record a per-employee risk (level, description, details, date) on the ordered scale low < need attention < medium < high < leaver, with retained history and no closable/terminal state — the level moves freely between any two states, including back down to low. Show a trend arrow versus the previous record, and provide a Risk Dashboard with severity-sorted counts and a drill-through table filterable by department/project/PP/manager, scoped to the viewer's Manager/PP access. "Active" (driving every dashboard counter) means any level above low. `leaver` is a prediction about someone still working and must never be conflated with the `dismissed` employment-status fact (CAP-14).
  - **success:** The current level is always the most recent record; the trend arrow appears only on an actual level change; active-risk counts everywhere exclude `low`; the section and dashboard are never visible to the employee themself; no report or query ever equates `leaver` with a recorded departure.

- **CAP-6 — Resourcing** *(Epic 6)*
  - **intent:** A DM/PM/permitted role creates a resourcing request — vacancy details, comp band (visible only to the author, the routed UM, and the reviewing DM; never PP, never in S15/a shared link/an export), duration, workload, headcount (default 1), and a required department that routes the request to its **current** UM, re-resolved on every view rather than pinned to whoever held that role at creation time (if re-resolution finds no successor, a proposal from a now-departed UM is flagged for reassignment); the project reference stays optional, and unattached requests surface in the DM dashboard's Unassigned bucket. A UM fulfils a routed request by proposing internal employees or an external candidate identified by PeopleForce candidate ID + link (full PeopleForce data pull is not required here — see CAP-13). Submitting an internal employee automatically generates a Shared Link (CAP-1) to their profile, naming the reviewing DM as sole recipient, scoped to S1/S4/S11/S12/S5(CV+certs only) with S6 optional and S2/S3/S7/S8 never included, and bound to the request's lifetime rather than a fixed clock. The DM approves/rejects each proposal with a written reason, and can later reverse an approval to rejected with a written reason, freeing the headcount slot it had filled; a request awaiting a decision from a now-departed DM auto-routes to a live DM on the same project, or is flagged for reassignment to a Full-access holder as backstop. Approval fills one headcount slot; a headcount edit that would drop below the already-filled count is rejected outright; approving a new candidate is blocked once remaining headcount is zero until the DM raises headcount or closes the request; only the DM's explicit close (permission-gated) ends the request, successfully or unsuccessfully — it never auto-closes on the last slot. Full history is retained on both the request and the employee's profile (S15). The resourcing request is a platform-owned entity, never synchronised with PeopleForce in either direction.
  - **success:** An unattached request with no project is a valid, normal state and appears in the Unassigned bucket; headcount filled/remaining is always accurate, never negative, and closing is never implicit; every proposal records proposed→approved/rejected with feedback, and a reversed approval correctly frees its slot; a departed UM's or DM's pending items are never left silently orphaned; the auto-generated shared link dies exactly when its request is decided; the comp band never leaks to S15, a shared link, an export, or the PP.

- **CAP-7 — Career Timeline** *(Epic 7)*
  - **intent:** Auto-generate a timeline event via one system-wide event-writer whenever a tracked change occurs (joining, grade/position/department change, FTE↔subcontractor transition, extended leave, mentorship pair start/end) — leaving the company is explicitly never a timeline event (it lives solely in CAP-14's employment status). Whoever holds the *edit the career timeline* permission can manually add/edit/delete events for historical backfill or correction. Presented as a readable chronological timeline on the profile.
  - **success:** Every tracked change type produces exactly one system-generated event with no separately maintained record; a manual correction is never silently overwritten by a later system-generated event covering the same window — the conflicting write is skipped and logged; no departure ever appears as a timeline event.

- **CAP-8 — CDS: Career Development System** *(Epic 8)*
  - **intent:** Each profile shows a resolved link to the current skills-matrix file for that person's department entity + position via a maintained dictionary keyed off the department entity (not a free-text string), an assessment log (date, assessor, result-file link, text conclusion) editable by whoever holds the *maintain CDS records* permission, and an IDP (description, deadline, external file link, complete checkbox) the employee can self-complete — plus All-Employees filters for last-assessment date (with "never assessed" as a distinct selectable option) and has-open-IDP.
  - **success:** Updating the centrally maintained matrix file, or renaming its owning department, changes what every affected profile's link resolves to, with no per-profile update needed; "never assessed" is selectable and distinct from any date-range result.

- **CAP-9 — Mentorship Hub** *(Epic 9)*
  - **intent:** An employee self-flags open-to-mentoring and sees their own mentor/mentees; whoever holds the *assign and end mentorships* permission browses a company-wide pool of everyone flagged open-to-mentor (identity-card data + flag only, never S13), assigns a mentor-mentee pair scoped to their own access — rejected server-side, at write time, unless the prospective mentor's open-to-mentoring flag is currently on, never relying on the pool UI alone to have filtered them out — (flipping the mentor's status to "mentor" on their first pair), ends a pair only with a required closure note recorded as its own field on the pair record — readable by the reporting line, project line, and PP only — rejecting the write if the pair was already ended by a concurrent request rather than silently double-logging the note, and views all pairs with dates. Clearing the open-to-mentor flag while holding an active mentee removes the person from the pool for future assignment without touching the active pair. The mentor is shown in every profile header alongside manager and PP, visible only per D5's stricter rule.
  - **success:** A pair cannot be ended without a closure note (except CAP-14's departure auto-close, which supplies a system-generated one, bypasses this gate, and is idempotent against an already-ended pair); a concurrent human attempt to end an already-ended pair surfaces a conflict rather than overwriting the note; a pair can never be created for a mentor whose consent flag is off, even via a direct API call; mentor-status transitions follow the self-flag rule exactly, even when the flag was turned off mid-pair; every pair end writes a CAP-7 timeline event; the pool never exposes S13 data.

- **CAP-10 — Forms & Survey Campaigns** *(Epic 10)*
  - **intent:** A PP/manager/permitted role creates a draft form campaign (title, description, purpose, external form link, due date), builds its audience via the CAP-2 filter engine or a saved view with individual add/remove, activates it to atomically freeze the audience and generate one CAP-4 action item per recipient, and tracks per-person completion (completed / not completed / overdue) — without ever reading or verifying the external form itself. A campaign is the only mechanism for distributing a form; requested feedback (CAP-11) reuses it rather than a second path.
  - **success:** The frozen audience never changes post-activation; activation is all-or-nothing, with no partial state; a recipient's own action-item completion is the only completion signal the system trusts; every distributed form traces back to exactly one campaign.

- **CAP-11 — Feedback** *(Epic 11)*
  - **intent:** Whoever holds the *create feedback* permission (by default the reporting/project line and PP) records a feedback record on a profile (subject, author, date, context, body) with a visibility flag defaulting to management-only and flippable to shared-with-employee, including joining-interview feedback (a feedback record, not a document, so the employee sees it only if explicitly shared). Records are listed chronologically and filterable by period — there is no period-over-period comparison, since the body is free text with nothing structural to compare. A requester runs a CAP-10 campaign targeted at named individuals to request feedback, then manually authors the resulting Feedback record after reading the external form's actual responses.
  - **success:** Flipping visibility makes a record immediately visible on the employee's own profile with no stale grant; a colleague never gets read access to feedback about someone else; completing a feedback-request campaign never auto-creates a Feedback record; joining-interview feedback never appears in S5.

- **CAP-12 — Dashboards** *(Epic 12)*
  - **intent:** One shared dashboard engine serving four role-scoped views — Unit Manager (grouped by people, active-risk-by-level and trend), Delivery Manager (grouped by project, with an all-projects/single-project selector plus an explicit Unassigned bucket for project-less requests, all folded into the all-projects counters), Project Manager (the DM view scoped to own projects), People Partner (grouped by department/project, no resourcing block) — each showing summary counters, a people/project table with risk (active-only) and leave status, and the viewer's own overdue-highlighted action items.
  - **success:** Selecting a project on the DM dashboard filters the whole page and every counter to it, including Unassigned as a selectable group; clearing the selection restores the all-projects aggregate, which always sums to the DM's actual request count; the PP dashboard never renders a resourcing block; no dashboard counts a `low` risk as active.

- **CAP-13 — External Integrations: Timetracker & PeopleForce** *(Epic 13)*
  - **intent:** Integrate the timetracker's Leaves API (S10) and Projects & People API (S11 — which also feeds the CAP-1 project-assignment access leg) against the provided test environment and seeded population, establishing whether the feed is event-based or state-snapshot-only; a new assignment confirms access the moment a single successful sync observes it (never requiring 15 continuous minutes of sustained success); on sync failure, serve last-known data behind a visible banner and withdraw project-derived access once cumulative failed sync time within a rolling window exceeds 4 hours — a flapping sync that never sustains 4 *continuous* failed hours still withdraws once its total failed time crosses the bound (see `decisions.md` D3, D19). PeopleForce integration is required only to the extent of storing a candidate's ID + link on a resourcing candidate (CAP-6); the full "Prefill from PeopleForce" button — one-way, read-only, per-field-confirmed, structurally excluding internal-decision fields (grade, seniority, employee type, department, manager, PP, contract, employment status, risk) — is a good-to-have, built only after required scope is done.
  - **success:** A timetracker sync outage fails safe for access (no access grant survives 4 cumulative hours of failed sync, whether continuous or flapping) and fails soft for display ("temporarily unavailable" instead of blocking the profile); a genuinely new assignment confirms even during a flapping sync window; a resourcing candidate's PeopleForce ID is always stored regardless of whether prefill is ever built; if built, prefill never writes an internal-decision field and never silently overwrites an existing value.

- **CAP-14 — Employment Status & Departure Lifecycle** *(Epic 14, new)*
  - **intent:** Maintain a time-bounded employment-status fact per employee (`active` | `dismissed`) as the single source every consumer reads — the departures figure in analytics, exclusion from the default All Employees view, dashboard counters — rather than each computing its own definition. Whoever holds the *record a departure* permission records one with an effective date and a reason; on that date the profile becomes read-only and drops from the default list (staying filterable), open action items auto-cancel as "cancelled — departed" (idempotently — a no-op if already cancelled by another path), active mentorship pairs auto-end with a system-generated closure note bypassing CAP-9's mandatory-note gate (idempotently — a no-op if the pair is already ended, covering both members of a pair departing on overlapping dates), every shared link the departing employee created is explicitly revoked as its own cascade step, the person's account deactivates, and every access grant they held ends immediately per CAP-1's standard revocation rule. Recording a departure for someone who still manages or partners anybody is blocked until they're re-parented, with re-parenting to their own manager offered as a one-click default on the same screen; recording a departure for the sole remaining full-access holder is blocked by the same gate until the grant is transferred.
  - **success:** Every departure consumer (analytics, default-list exclusion, dashboard counts) agrees, because all read the same record; a manager/PP departure, or the sole full-access holder's departure, can never be recorded while orphaning their reports or the full-access grant; the cascade (action items, mentorship, shared links, account, access) is atomic, immediate, and safe under repetition — never a lingering background job and never a double-write when two cascade triggers overlap.

## Constraints

- Access roles (derived from relationships, never assigned), functional roles (assigned, extensible, data not code, never widen data access), and full profile access (a separate, non-bundled, non-self-assignable grant) are three independent dimensions and must never collapse into one role list. HR Admin, specifically, grants no data access on its own.
- A write is permitted only where **both** the section-access matrix (audience → section RW/R/none) and the role-permission matrix (functional role → feature permission) allow it — a section's `RW` for an audience is not itself a blanket grant for every permission-gated action inside it.
- Manager access is the transitive closure of **three** relations — reports-to, department-management (nested), and project-assignment — not two. Reports-to and department-management form the reporting line (full manager grant); project-assignment alone forms the project line, which is strictly narrower: no S2, no S3, S5 limited to CV+certificates. *(access-model.md Rule 2)*
- The employee's manager, the employee's PP, the employee's department, and a department's manager are access-switch fields, writable only through a dedicated *change organisational relationships* permission on a dedicated screen — never through the general profile-edit path, never self-assignable regardless of held permissions, always journaled. *(access-model.md, spec 2.1/3.4)* Four write-time guards close concurrency and cycle gaps: a department's manager may never be a current member of that department or a nested sub-department; a manager change is rejected if it would create a reporting cycle; a concurrent conflicting write to the same field is rejected with a conflict error, never silently overwritten; clearing a department's manager is blocked unless a replacement is designated in the same change. *(D15, `decisions.md`)*
- Full profile access is granted only by an existing holder, is never self-assignable, is seeded at deployment, and can never be revoked down to zero holders — the holder count is re-checked at commit time, never from a stale read, closing the concurrent-mutual-revocation race; recording a departure for the sole remaining holder is blocked by the same never-zero gate CAP-14 applies to managers/PP. *(D16, `decisions.md`)*
- When a viewer's relationships resolve to more than one audience for the same subject (e.g. both PP and Project line), effective access per section is the union — the least-restrictive access among all resolved audiences for that section — never a single ranked precedence. *(D13, `decisions.md`; access-model.md Rule 10)*
- Access is evaluated server-side, per section, per request — never cached client-side, never assumed stable across a session; any permission cache invalidates synchronously off a generation-bump on the relationship graph, never relying on TTL for correctness. *(D1, `decisions.md`)* Platform-owned relationship changes take effect on the very next request; project-assignment changes take effect within 15 minutes — both are access-control guarantees, not display-freshness ones.
- The colleague view is a strict server/API-level whitelist (S1, S10 dates only with leave type hidden, S11 project name only) — never implemented by hiding fields client-side. Its one sanctioned widening is the campaign-sender exception (a campaign creator sees that campaign's own recipients and that campaign's own action-item status, nothing else, ending when the campaign closes); no other feature may widen it without a new product decision.
- Custom fields carry their own visibility (management default / employee / colleague); filters and list columns must respect it — a user must never infer a value they can't see via a filter.
- Any profile field, including runtime-defined custom fields, must be filterable/sortable/column-usable with no schema migration — a column-per-field data model is rejected. The manager/PP/department fields are filterable but never inline-editable from the directory grid.
- Grade, position, department, employment type, and employment status are all temporal — modeled as time-bounded records, not scalar fields plus a bolted-on audit log.
- CDS never encodes the competency matrix in schema — it's a registry/hub only; assessment and the matrix itself happen outside the system. The matrix dictionary keys off the department entity, never a free-text department string.
- The system never reads or verifies an external form or its responses, for campaigns or feedback requests alike — the assignee's own action-item completion is the only trusted completion signal. A campaign is the sole distribution mechanism for any external form; no second path is permitted.
- A resourcing request is valid and normal with no linked project — project attachment must not be required; unattached requests surface in an explicit Unassigned bucket rather than being invisible. A resourcing request is a platform-owned entity and is never synchronised with PeopleForce in either direction.
- An external candidate on a resourcing request always carries a stored PeopleForce candidate ID + link, regardless of whether the full PeopleForce prefill integration (CAP-13, good-to-have) is ever built.
- Cross-system identity resolves via an explicit `(system, externalId) → employeeId` mapping table, never inferred from email alone, with an explicit `supersededBy` pointer for re-hires; the platform's own seeded record, not a PeopleForce candidate, is the primary identity anchor (§4.17) — a PeopleForce link is optional per employee.
- A resourcing approval never itself writes a `ProjectAssignment` (C3) record — the person is assigned to the project in the timetracker, and CAP-13's sync is the only writer; CAP-6's approval action only records the approval decision and fills a headcount slot.
- Leave requests, edits, and balances are never performed or shown in-app — CAP-3 displays an employee's own leave data read-only via S10 (colleagues get dates only, type hidden, per the matrix) and links out to the timetracker for balance management; the timetracker is the only place leave balances live or change.
- A shared link works only for an authenticated, explicitly-named recipient — never anonymous. Its creator's own access is re-checked on every view — a continuous, per-section re-clamp to the creator's currently-held access, not a one-time or alive/dead check, so a section stops rendering the instant the creator's access to it narrows *(D14, `decisions.md`)*; revocation and journal-read rights follow whoever currently holds Manager/PP access over the subject (full-access holders as backstop), not the original creator — there must never be a link nobody can revoke. Every shared link a departing employee created is explicitly revoked as a named step of CAP-14's departure cascade, not left to the re-clamp mechanism alone *(D17)*. The never-share set is {S3, S7, S13, S14}; sensitive sections {S2, S5, S6, S8} must be explicitly re-enabled on every link; only S1 is on by default.
- Employment status (`active`/`dismissed`) is the single source of truth for "has this person left" across every consumer — analytics, default-list exclusion, dashboard counts — and must never be conflated with the unrelated `leaver` risk-level prediction.
- Team decomposition and delivery sequencing must guarantee zero cross-person blocking — one developer waiting on another is a process defect regardless of shipped output. Cross-feature dependencies resolve only via a frozen interface contract, a stub, a spec-sanctioned fallback, or same-developer sequencing.
- BMad is the mandated planning/process framework for at least the start of the project; migrating away from it must be a recorded deliberate decision, never silent drift.
- Non-production environments use only the seeded test population (§4.17) — never real employee data, never real personal data in agent contexts, logs, screenshots, or the repository.
- The All Employees list responds within 2 seconds at 500+ records under arbitrary filters, including permission-resolution cost.
- External integration failures (timetracker, PeopleForce) degrade gracefully and never take the application down, within the explicit bounds stated for the timetracker (D3: 4-hour access withdrawal on sync failure, accumulated across a rolling window rather than reset by brief recovery, D19; a single successful sync confirms a new assignment, D19).
- List, profile, and dashboard pages meet accessibility (WCAG 2.1 AA baseline, see `decisions.md` appendix) and responsive-layout requirements.
- Access-control correctness is the system's primary quality attribute — every audience/relationship-path/section combination, every `—` cell and narrowed cell, every flag-gated record against both gated audiences, the campaign-sender exception, and every self-assignment/journal guarantee must be covered by an explicit test.
- The intelligent repository (this workspace: specs, decisions, call transcripts, external API docs, agent rules/skills) is mandatory, not optional documentation — and a foundation phase (prototyping/design approach, best-practice research, test architecture, named owners per topic) runs before feature implementation starts, with findings aligned and written down first. Inter-team communication and status are captured for analysis.

## Non-goals

- Compensation and salary data on the employee profile, this or any planned iteration — a resourcing request's expected-compensation band is a distinct, in-scope object (CAP-6), not this.
- Employee provisioning — no creation flow, no Active Directory integration; the population is seeded only (§4.17).
- SSO — no Entra ID this iteration; authentication is the platform's own implementation over the seeded population.
- Leave balances shown in-platform — self-service links out to the timetracker for both viewing leaves and managing balances.
- Vacancy synchronisation with PeopleForce — the resourcing request has no counterpart in the recruiting system, in either direction.
- Pre-onboarding — creating a person before their first working day and pulling ATS data on offer acceptance — deferred.
- Email template management (eSender replacement) — deferred.
- Performing competency assessments inside the system — links to matrices and records outcomes only; assessment happens outside.
- Learning management (LMS) functionality — a separate track, not duplicated here.
- Mentorship goals, session logs, and progress tracking — pair formation/ending/visibility only, this iteration.
- Project allocation percentages / workload distribution modeling — an employee's ongoing % allocation across projects is not tracked or computed. (A resourcing request's one-time *requested* workload value, CAP-6, is a distinct, in-scope field — not the same concept.)
- In-app/email notifications — not required this iteration (good-to-have, build only once core scope is complete).
- Analytics and reports — not required this iteration (good-to-have, build only once core scope is complete).

## Success signal

The platform is deployed and demonstrable — not running on someone's laptop — with every access-matrix cell (including the narrowed project-line cells and the campaign-sender exception) and negative case covered by passing tests, organisational-relationship changes and full-access grants provably non-self-assignable and journal-visible, a shared link provably working only for its named authenticated recipient and always revocable by whoever currently holds the relationship, a new functional role provably creatable and grantable through the UI with no deploy, and the timetracker integration running against its provided test environment over the seeded population. The intelligent repository's specs match shipped behavior, and the delivery shows no developer-waits-on-developer defect (per Constraints), verified against whatever wave/track plan the `bmad-sprint-planning` phase produces — that plan is out-of-contract input to this success signal, not something this SPEC defines itself.

## Assumptions

- A manually edited timeline event is never silently overwritten by a later system-generated event covering the same change window — the system write is skipped and logged (documented as an ADR). *(D2, `decisions.md`)*
- Mentor status reverts to "open to mentoring" when the last active pair ends only if the self-flag is still on; otherwise it reverts to no status; un-flagging mid-pair never touches an active pair. *(D4)*
- Mentorship-end closing feedback is stored as its own field on the mentorship pair record, readable only by the reporting line, project line, and PP. *(D6)*
- IDP maintenance (manager/PP side) and the employee's self-complete checkbox are owned by the same story/developer rather than split across epics. *(D7)*
- The `external_identities` mapping table shape is an early-ratified starting point, refinable once the real PeopleForce/timetracker investigation (Epic 13) completes; the platform's own seeded record, not a PeopleForce candidate, is the primary identity anchor. *(D8/C5)*
- Nine further low-stakes defaults ship as-is unless told otherwise — upload limits, IDP reopening, saved-view shape, CDS assessor field, timeline-deletion mode, post-access-loss cancellation, link expiry (with the resourcing-generated exception), the accessibility conformance target, and saved-view ownership on the creator's departure (becomes ownerless, not auto-transferred — no clean manager/PP-of-a-view concept exists).
